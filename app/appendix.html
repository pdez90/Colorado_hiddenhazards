Appendix: assumptions, decisions and limitations

Every assumption, judgement call and known limitation behind this site, in the order the data are built. Each row is labelled: Assumption something taken as true without being checked for every record; Decision a choice between defensible alternatives (dated where the author made it); Limitation a known way the data fall short, with its size where it has been measured; Check a test of the data against another source; Note a fact needed to read the others. Figures are for the inventory rebuilt on 18 November 2026 (13 complete editions) unless stated. Click a heading to open it.

1. What is countedsources, editions and the hazard definition
ItemKindWhat it means
Only the 13 complete editions of the Directory of Colorado Manufacturers are used: 1965, 1966, 1967, 1968, 1970, 1973, 1974, 1977, 1980, 1983, 1988, 1990, 1992DecisionThe other 12 (1961, 1962, 1971, 1976, 1979, 1982, 1984, 1985, 1986, 1993, 1999, 2002) reached the project as scans that stop part-way through the geographic section. They are kept in a separate all-editions file (Data & downloads) and enter no result.
The directory is a voluntary listing of manufacturersAssumptionA plant never listed is absent: very small shops, firms that declined to answer the publisher, and non-manufacturing hazards (dry cleaners, gas stations, auto repair, rail yards). The inventory is a floor, not a census. Against County Business Patterns the complete editions list 0.75–1.19 times the number of manufacturing establishments (Benchmarks tab).
Nothing after 1992LimitationFirms that opened after 1992 are absent. Analyses that need "firms of the decade" for 2000, 2010 or 2020 use the last complete decade, 1983–1992, so those years measure proximity to historically listed sites, not to active manufacturing.
Firms are seen only in edition yearsLimitationA firm that opened and closed between two editions (say 1977 and 1980) is never seen. A site's first and last years are the first and last editions it appears in, not its true dates.
"Firm of a decade"DecisionA 1965–92 site counts for a census when it has a hazardous-sector listing in the ten years ending at that census. A pre-1960 firm, whose editions are sparse, counts in every decade between its first and last listing. The 1880 districts use the firms of the 1878–85 editions.
Hazardous = Frickel and Elliott's seven sectorsDecisionSIC 28 chemicals, 29 petroleum refining, 30 rubber and plastics, 33 primary metals, 34 fabricated metals, 35 industrial machinery, 37 transportation equipment (NAICS 325, 324, 326, 331, 332, 333, 336). The same definition selects the pre-1960 trades, the 1965–92 sites and the firms in every model and map.
A sector is a proxy for hazardAssumptionNo record says what a firm stored, used or released. A machine shop and a smelter count once each. Group 35 includes computer and office-equipment assembly, which counts like the rest of the group.
"Extended sectors"DecisionWood preserving, tanning, electronic components and circuit boards, batteries, photographic chemicals and ore milling are flagged separately and shown only when chosen in the sidebar; they are not in the headline counts.
Ordnance in the 1957 code systemDecisionThe 1965–68 editions file ordnance under major group 19; from 1972 it is 348x. Codes 19xx are counted as group 34.
The pre-1960 layer: 34 editions of the Colorado State Business Directory, 1871–1956Note1871, 1875, 1877, 1878, 1879, 1880, 1881, 1882, 1883, 1884, 1885, 1887, 1888, 1891, 1892, 1910–1917, 1920, 1927, 1929, 1932, 1933, 1935, 1939, 1941, 1943, 1950, 1956. None survives for 1893–1909; the decade ending in 1910 has 1910 only. Counts of firms are therefore not compared across censuses.
2. Reading the scansOCR, parsing, the scan review and spelling
ItemKindWhat it means
Text is read by OCR (Tesseract 5.3.4, word boxes) and split into listings by format-specific parsersAssumptionEach edition's layout (columns, line pitch, where the address and code sit) is assumed. Column cuts are chosen per page among the detected and forced two- or three-column splits by whichever leaves the fewest over-long lines; lines are clustered on the printed line pitch. Pages that break the layout give merged or split listings.
OCR digit confusions are corrected from contextDecisionC→0, e/€/@→8, B→8 in codes and ZIPs; S/$→5, E→1, G→0 in house numbers. A five-digit token becomes a ZIP only when it is a Colorado ZIP and not the house number. A ZIP left at the end of the street is moved back to the ZIP field.
A page scanned twice within an edition is parsed once; a record without a firm name is attached to its listing or droppedDecisionFrom the 13 November re-parse. Town headings contradicted by the towns printed under them are replaced (in 1992 the OCR dropped the BOULDER heading and filed its firms under Black Hawk).
A re-read of the same scan is not letter-for-letter identicalLimitationThe page image depends on the PDF library's version; in the 18 November re-read about 200 listings of 1983 were spelled slightly differently. Without the scan review the rebuild reproduced the 13 November run to within 4 sites of 3,565.
Full scan review, 14 November 2026DecisionAll 10,586 hazardous-sector listings on 1,191 pages were read against the page image: 8,091 correct, 2,494 corrected (name, street or town), 1 not a listing. After the author's decisions of 18 November, 260 are judged not hazardous. 10,565 review rows are applied, 200 of them by a near match on the same page (name similarity ≥ 80 of 100, or name plus street ≥ 165 of 200); 21 could not be matched after the re-read.
The review was done by AI readers working from a written protocolLimitationA blind second reading of 150 listings agreed on the hazardous verdict and the printed codes in all 150, on the overall verdict in 143 and on street corrections in 146.
Only hazardous-sector listings were reviewedLimitationA hazardous firm that the software read as non-hazardous (a misread code) was never reviewed and is missing. The review removes false positives; it cannot find false negatives.
Spelling clean-up across editionsDecision (18 Nov)A misread firm or street name is replaced by the spelling that a strict majority of at least three listings of the same firm (or the same street address and town) agree on: 5,552 names and 1,277 street names respelled. Numbers, one- and two-letter street words (directions), a word that differs by more than a misreading, and listings with a different number of words are never changed; a renamed firm keeps its new name. The reading as scanned is kept in name_as_read and street_as_read.
Names listed in fewer than three editions stay as readDecision (18 Nov)One-off misreadings remain. In the author's check of 65 listings, 9 names (14%) had misread letters; all were the right firm. Names enter no count.
Map points use the address as read, not the cleaned spellingDecisionSo the displayed street and the point never disagree. In the author's check, 4 of 5 misspelled addresses sat on the same point as the clean spelling.
Pre-1960 text layers are uncorrected OCR with columns interleavedLimitationNames can be garbled and an entry can run into its neighbour's. 1871, 1875 and 1877 are read from Internet Archive / Google Books copies; the CU 1885 and 1920 scans had no text layer and were OCR'd here (300 and 220 dpi).
3. Industry codesprinted, corrected and inferred
ItemKindWhat it means
The printed code decidesDecisionWhere the directory prints a SIC (1965, 1967–1993) or NAICS (2002) code, that code is used even if the product line reads oddly, with the three exceptions below.
Three printed codes overruled as misprintsDecision (18 Nov)Bestway Concrete, Greeley (1990, 1992): printed 3773 "ready mix concrete", read as 3273, not hazardous. Sew Be It, Littleton (1992): printed 3069 with "baby clothes", not hazardous. Chinook Technology, Longmont (1992): printed 3751 "hard drives", read as 3572, still hazardous.
Automatic code correctionsDecisionA printed code whose printed description belongs to a code one digit away is corrected ("2911 meat packing" → 2011; 876 codes over all 25 editions). A hazardous code that contradicts the firm's own name or description is removed (a bakery with "paving mixtures"; 290 listings). Both are logged in the downloads.
Editions without printed codesLimitation1961, 1962 and 1966 print a product description only, and 1999 prints codes for few listings. Codes are inferred by a nearest-neighbour classifier trained on description–code pairs from the other editions, plus keyword rules (smelters, refineries, plating, wood preserving). In the scan review 6.8% of 1966's hazardous listings were judged not hazardous, against under 1.5% in most editions with printed codes.
1965 is the least reliable complete edition for codesLimitationIts codes are printed without a description, so a misread code cannot be checked against one. A code is kept only if it is one the directory uses, repaired when exactly one valid code fits, otherwise dropped and inferred from the name. 14.2% of its hazardous listings were judged not hazardous in the scan review and are removed. The repair uses the classifier, whose answers differ slightly between versions of the scikit-learn library.
Evidence labelNoteEach listing records whether its code was printed or inferred (industry_code_source); codes confirmed in the scan review count as printed.
4. Geocodinghow every layer was put on the map
4a. The 1965–1992 manufacturers' directory
ItemKindWhat it means
Addresses are placed on today's street networkAssumptionHouse numbers are interpolated along the US Census Bureau's TIGER/Line 2023 address ranges (ADDRFEAT), offline and deterministically. A street renamed, renumbered or removed since the listing is misplaced or not placed.
Interpolation is not a rooftopLimitationThe point is where the number would fall if numbers were spread evenly along the block, not the building.
Two precision levels count as locatedDecisionaddress: exact match of number, street, direction and type in the stated ZIP or town. address_approx: any loosened match — street-type or direction misread, unit numbers or trailing text dropped, a fuzzy street name (similarity ≥ 86, keeping the street's own numbers), a neighbouring ZIP within 12 km, or a missing direction settled by rule. Both form sites; the level is in every download and drives the "confidence" label.
Missing directions in Denver-grid townsDecision"4300 Lipan" can be north or south. The printed street type decides first, then the printed ZIP's own address ranges or its half of the grid. An address neither settles is left unplaced (kept at ZIP level, flagged dirambig).
BaselinesDecisionA prefixed address is never placed on the unprefixed street across the Ellsworth and Broadway baselines; a final check sets to unlocated any Denver listing whose point lies on the wrong side for its own direction (none remain). "SO"/"NO" and a direction letter glued to the street name are read as directions.
City checkDecisionA match more than 15 km from the stated town is rejected. Listings filed under the wrong town heading (sometimes in 1961, 1962 and 1965) fail this check and stay off the map.
Each point is offset 12 m from the centrelineDecisionToward the side of the street whose range held the number, so the two sides of a street stay apart.
One-digit ruleDecision (18 Nov)An unplaced listing whose street differs in exactly one inner digit from a placed listing of the same firm in the same town takes that listing's point ("12280 W 90th Pl" for "12280 W 50th Pl"): 73 listings, 40 hazardous. A difference in the last digit (450 / 452) is not treated as a misreading, because it can be the next building; a listing left unplaced for an ambiguous direction is not moved either.
Listings with no street-level point are in no siteLimitation5,225 of 15,575 hazardous-sector listings (34%) fall back to a ZIP or town centre: rural routes, PO boxes, misread numbers, unmatched streets. They count as listings, never as sites, so site counts are a floor. Coverage varies by edition: 30–65% of listings at address level, 40–77% including approximate.
Star-route and rural addressesLimitationAddresses such as "296 Sandy … SR" west of Boulder (Clark Associates, 1988) are placed approximately and may be several hundred metres off; left as is by the author's decision.
Check against Colorado's statewide address points (October 2026, 1,000 random sites)Checkaddress level: median 49 m, 79% within 100 m, 94% within 250 m, 0.9% over 1 km (n = 455 compared). address_approx: median 103 m, 50% within 100 m, 75% within 250 m, 10.1% over 1 km (n = 119). A re-check on 17 October gave median 67 m and 88% within 250 m at address level. About a third of address-level listings have no address point today (building gone, street renumbered, or misread number) and could not be checked. The State's service was unreachable on 18 November, so the check was not rerun on the rebuilt data.
Check against EPA Facility Registry coordinates for identical addressesCheckaddress level, n = 1,238: median 48 m, 88% within 250 m, 3.1% over 1 km (FRS coordinates carry their own error).
Check against the scans (25 random sites)Check23 matched the printed page; one house number was misread (2915 for 3915 Tennyson, about 1.3 km off). Expect roughly 4% of house numbers to carry an OCR digit error.
Five unresolved north/south casesLimitation"4440 Yates", "4300 Lipan", "4200 Fox", "4450 Cherokee", "4010 Holly": placed on the north side (their printed ZIPs are north-Denver ZIPs) while the State's points know only the south-side address, 14–16 km away. Left at address level.

ItemKindWhat it means
The town of each entryAssumptionFrom town headings ("ALPINE.", "DENVER CONT'D"), page running heads and, failing that, a town named in the entry, matched to the USGS GNIS gazetteer. Town names shared by two places (Alpine, Gold Hill, Independence, Colorado City …) are resolved by a hand-made table for the years each can mean.

Later street renamings are not handledLimitationPoints use today's street geometry; they were checked against the page text, not against historical maps.
Vanished mining campsLimitation104 reviewed listings in camps with no coordinates even in the 2021 GNIS archive (Mosquito, Dora, Robinson, Parrot City …) stay unplaced and out of the counts. Older names of surviving places (Animas City, Oro City) are mapped by hand.

Office listings and workers at home addressesDecisionVetting removed 2,205 entries that were workers listed at their homes ("stoker, Denver Gas Co, 2422 Champa") and stopped placing 1,846 office listings at their downtown offices.

ItemKindWhat it means

Firms of 1878–1885 are placed on the 1880 grid by their printed house numbersDecisionAt the building with that number, else the nearest number on the street, else the street only: 64 of 84 firm listings placed (29 at a building, 29 at the nearest number, 6 street only; street-only placements are left out of the district analysis).
4d. Enumeration districts 1900–1940 (reconstructed)
ItemKindWhat it means
No public map of 1900–1940 districts existsNoteIPUMS gives each person's district number but no address. Brown's Geographic Reference Files list every household's street, house number and district (no coordinates).
Every distinct address is geocoded with the same offline TIGER geocoderAssumptionDenver's post-1887 numbering still applies; renamed streets are translated. Only street-level results are kept: 72% of the 1900 addresses.
Stray geocodes are droppedDecisionAn address farther from its district's person-weighted median centre than max(400 m, 2.5 × the median), or with fewer than 30% of its ten nearest neighbours in the same district. The addresses kept hold 60% of the 1900 population (73% in 1940).
Districts are drawn by Voronoi tessellationDecisionCells around the kept addresses are dissolved by district and clipped to the concave hull of all addresses; each district keeps its main body and freed ground goes to the nearest district, so districts are space-filling like tracts. 1880 is drawn the same way from Brown's buildings.
Boundaries are approximateLimitationAbout a block at the edges. Populations come from the IPUMS full count joined by district number (1900–1930: ENUMDIST mod 100000; 1940: supervisor district, district and suffix, e.g. "250A"); mapped populations reproduce the county totals (317,663 of 322,780 in 1940).
Districts are not linked across censusesLimitationEach census has its own districts. Where they are compared with HOLC areas, grid cells or 2010 tracts, counts are area-weighted onto those units.
4e. 1940 residents followed to death and enlistment (CenSoc)
ItemKindWhat it means
Each person's 1940 address comes from Brown's GRF, geocoded as in 4dAssumptionExposures (1938 HOLC grade, 1929 zoning district, industrial and commercial land within 250 m in 1937, hazardous trades listed to 1940 within 500 m and as a 500 m kernel, 1965–92 sites) are measured at that point. People whose address did not geocode at street level are not in the models.
Links are CenSoc'sAssumptionCenSoc links the 1940 census to Social Security death records (Numident: men and women, deaths 1988–2005; DMF: men, deaths from 1975) and to WWII enlistment records. Linked samples are mostly men, cover only deaths in those windows, and are reweighted with CenSoc's weights; they are not representative of all 1940 residents.
Earlier addresses (1910–1930) come from the IPUMS Multigenerational Longitudinal PanelAssumptionBecause surviving directory years list very different numbers of sites, exposure at those addresses is also expressed as a percentile among all geocoded Denver residents of the same census.
4f. Deaths 2015–2019 (Colorado death records)
ItemKindWhat it means
Deaths are placed by the 2010 block code in the state's fileAssumptionThe project does not geocode death addresses; it uses the block assigned in the record. Residence at death, not residential history.
Not every death can be usedLimitationOf 189,606 deaths, 78.7% are in the 59,293 blocks the Colorado block models use; 16.4% are in blocks with fewer than 100 person-years (or missing an age structure, PM2.5 or tract poverty); 4.9% carry a block code not among the 2010 blocks. Block-group models keep 95% of natural-cause deaths.

ItemKindWhat it means

ItemKindWhat it means

5. Siteswhat a "site" is
ItemKindWhat it means
A site is a cluster of listings within 30 m, not a parcelDecision (kept 18 Nov)Next-door premises are counted as one site, so on streets of small workshops the site count is lower than the number of premises. Numbered addresses on opposite sides of the same street are not joined directly.
ChainsLimitationClustering is by connection: a row of workshops each within 30 m of the next becomes one site even if its ends are further apart. In the author's check of 40 sites none mixed distant addresses (largest spread 21 m); about a dozen held neighbouring numbers (5612 Newland Way, Arvada holds about ten).
A site is hazardous if any listing there isDecisionSuccessive tenants and renamed firms share a site; it is hazardous if any listing, in any complete edition, is in a hazardous sector.
Linking listings into establishmentsAssumptionAcross editions by token-sorted name similarity ≥ 92 within a ZIP or town, or ≥ 80 at the same street address: 45,715 listings → 22,060 establishments. A badly misread name can fail to link, which overstates the number of establishments at a site.
Phantom sites from misread numbersCheckBefore the scan review one firm could appear as several sites (Western Motor Rebuilders at 20, 520 and 620 W 12th Ave). Correcting streets removed most of about 420 sites (3,561 → 3,141). Listings outside the hazardous sectors were not reviewed, so the same error persists there; it does not affect hazardous-site counts.
Site identifiers are not permanentLimitationIdentifiers (S00001 …) are renumbered whenever the inventory is rebuilt.
6. Hidden or knownmatching sites to today's inventories
ItemKindWhat it means
"Not on any list" is not "contaminated"NoteIt means no record on the searched federal and state lists could be tied to the site. It is a lead for records review or soil testing, not evidence of contamination, and not evidence that no agency knows of it.
Lists searchedDecisionEPA's Facility Registry Service (Colorado; all programmes), EPA TRI, and CDPHE's hazardous-materials and waste layers: Superfund/SEMS, brownfields, voluntary cleanup, federal facilities, hazardous-waste handlers and corrective action, institutional controls (environmental covenants), solid waste, uranium mill tailings.
Lists not searchedLimitationState petroleum-tank and release registers are reported separately and do not make a site known. Local fire-department, county and city records are not searched.
The lists are a snapshot of 18 November 2026LimitationThey change. A rebuild with freshly downloaded lists moved the hidden count by 9 of 1,284 against the 13 November run.
A site is known if any of four tests holdsDecisionA record within 75 m (cleanup programmes), 100 m (TRI) or 40 m (hazardous-waste handlers and other permits); a record of the same firm name within 250 m; a record at the same address; or the point lies inside a mapped cleanup boundary. A site's radius grows with the spread of its own points, to at most 250 m.
Name matchingAssumptionTwo names match with two shared informative words or a near-identical whole name. A firm that changed its name before reaching a list is missed; one that shares two words with an unrelated neighbour is matched.
"Known" often rests on a nearby record onlyLimitation916 of the 2,120 known sites (43%) are known only because a differently named record lies within the radius, which may belong to another occupant of the block. The hidden share is therefore near the low end of what reasonable rules give: 18% with every radius doubled, 62% counting only the same firm, the same address or a cleanup boundary. Counting the roughly 90 address-only matches with no other nearby record as hidden would raise 32.5% to about 35%.
Reviewed matches beyond the rulesDecision (18 Nov)A search out to 400 m found 65 hidden sites with a record of a similar name or the same street number. 25 now count as known: 12 with the same firm name and street address whose two points are 250–400 m apart, and 13 judged the same place by the author. They are in a reviewed file applied by the pipeline.
The other 40 candidatesLimitationNot individually examined; some may be the same place and are still counted as hidden.
No one checked each hidden site against the agencies' own search pagesLimitationAn October check of 50 hidden sites against the directory text found about 1 in 10 with a doubtful industry code.
HeadlineNote3,141 hazardous-sector sites, 1,021 (32.5%) on no list; Denver 972 sites, 235 on no list. Before the scan review: 3,565 and 1,284 (36.0%).

8. Census units, redlining, zoning and land usehow places are compared over time
ItemKindWhat it means
One fixed tract study areaDecisionThe 147 census tracts (2010 boundaries) whose centroid lies within 10 km of downtown Denver. Tract-years with fewer than 200 residents are dropped from every measure; the tracted area grows (97 tracts in 1950, 144 in 1960, 145–147 from 1970), so trends are also shown on fixed sets of tracts.
Earlier tracts moved onto 2010 tracts by area weightingAssumptionPeople are assumed spread evenly within an old tract. Counts are interpolated first and shares computed afterwards, each with its own table's denominator; medians are averaged with weights of occupied units times overlap. 1990–2020 population, race and Hispanic origin come from NHGIS's standardised series instead.
Hispanic origin is measured differently over timeLimitation1880–1940 districts use IPUMS HISPAN, inferred from birthplace, parents' birthplace and Spanish surname; tracts use Spanish surname (1960), Spanish American (1970), Spanish origin (1980) and Hispanic (1990+). The two series are compared in direction, not level. The 1940 tract tables report nonwhite rather than Black.
Questions a census did not ask are missing, not zeroDecisionOwnership in 1880, SEI in the 1900 extract, rent and residence five years earlier only in 1940; 1940 sample-line questions (about 5% of persons) are left missing at district level.
1940 contract rentDecision (18 Nov)About 3% of 1940 renter households in the Colorado full count carry the rent multiplied by 100 (1500 for $15.00); in enumeration districts 238 and 243 nearly every value does, which had given medians of $2,000 and $3,300 a month. Values of 500 or more are divided by 100; the few of 200–499 cannot be told apart and are set to missing. With the correction, the median rent was lower in the 1940 districts with a hazardous firm ($17 against $27 a month), the opposite of the uncorrected figures.
Counties 1880–1940LimitationShown on each census's own county boundaries and not harmonised (counties were created and split through 1913).
Redlining grades are the 1938 map'sAssumption"Before the map" comparisons assign the 1938 grade areas to earlier years. HOLC graded only residential areas; 16 non-residential zones were outlined but labelled Industrial or Commercial, not graded.
The simplified tract layer does not tile DenverLimitationThe tract outlines used in the app and some analyses leave 91 sliver gaps inside Denver County (0.72 km²); a point on a tract boundary can fall in no tract (7 rezonings placed at street crossings get no tract).
Present-day status scores overlap with the outcomeLimitationEnviroScreen already includes proximity to listed sites, so comparing hidden sites with that score is partly circular.
Spatial unitNoteEvery card on this site states its spatial unit (site, block, block group, tract, district, grid cell, HOLC area). Results are not comparable across units.
9. Health and mortalityassociations, not effects
ItemKindWhat it means
Exposure is a kernel density of site pointsAssumptionGaussian, 500 m bandwidth, 2 km cutoff, per standard deviation. A site counts the same whatever it made, however long it operated and whatever it released, and carries the location error of section 4.
Ecological designLimitationRates are compared across areas with controls for age structure, socioeconomic status, density and PM2.5. Unmeasured differences between neighbourhoods can produce or hide an association.
Residence at death, not residential historyLimitationPeople who lived near a site for decades and moved away are not counted there.
Tract health outcomes vary only between tractsLimitationEnviroScreen 2.0 repeats the tract value for every block group; heart disease, cancer, diabetes and mental-health indices are modelled estimates.
PlaceboDecisionNon-hazardous manufacturers from the same directories (same source, same geocoding, no hazardous process).
Death records stay on the author's machineDecisionOnly rate ratios and aggregates leave it, and no cell under 11 deaths is published.
RebuildingAssumptionA site counts as rebuilt from the year its parcel's present structure was built, when that is after its last listing; a parcel without a building date is kept as standing. Rebuilding also brings younger residents, whose age the population offset does not capture.
Survival-model (Gompertz) checkDecisionCenSoc's doubly-truncated Gompertz model is fitted by Nelder–Mead, which can stop at its iteration limit or short of the optimum when there are many controls; each fit is restarted from its own estimates until it has converged and the likelihood no longer improves.
